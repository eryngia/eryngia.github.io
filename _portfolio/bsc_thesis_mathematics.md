My mathematics thesis, supervised by Prof Jonathan Fraser, examines how the dimension of self-similar fractals can be calculated beyond the standard Euclidean spaces.

The thesis discusses the Hausdorff dimension, a well-behaved notion of dimension for fractals based on the Hausdorff measure that permits non-integer values.  It then develops the theory of iterated function systems as way to encode unique fractal attractors and define self-similar sets.

The main results concern self-similar sets. The Hausdorff dimension is equal to their similarity values under suitable separation conditions. The thesis rigorously proves this in complete metric spaces under the strong open set condition and in complete doubling spaces under the open set condition, where the dimension is also shown to be strict. 

The results are applied to Sierpiński polygons, for which I derived the scaling ratio and the iterated function system for an arbitrary number of sides. I analysed how the resulting dimension changes with this number.
