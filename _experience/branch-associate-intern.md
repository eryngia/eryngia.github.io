I spent the summer in a retail branch in Leuven, assisting a team of five banking advisors with customer inquiries and the paperwork behind them. Most of my time was spent preparing and checking documentation under the bank's know-your-customer and data protection requirements, which meant that every file needed verified identification, signed consents, and an accurate record before it could be approved. I also helped coordinate between different departments.

One particular task was noticably inefficient. When a young customer turned 18, their youth account had to be converted into a standard current account, which in Belgium requires an identity check and updated consent. Advisors had to handle this manually by individually checking client details, calling them or their parents multiple times to arrange a meeting, and personally printing and signing multiple documents. There were hundreds of such cases each year, so the work was substantial and often ignored.

With my supervisor's agreement, I built a small tool that read the branch's monthly database export of upcoming 18th birthdays, converted them to an Excel document, and created letters or app alerts filled in with the relevant information. I independently researched company style guidelines to ensure quality communication, since the templates did not exist yet. The time to process this issue for each customer fell from around half an hour to around 5 minutes. My supervisor asked to adopt this tool for the future, and I documented its funtionality so it could be used by other associates.
