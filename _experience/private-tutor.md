I tutored a 14-year-old student with dyslexia and dyscalculia, in two 90-minute sessions per week covering maths, science, French, and Dutch. I arranged the work myself, from scheduling and materials to reporting progress to the parents every two weeks.

The student was discouraged after years of falling behind, so I focused on building confidence by starting with manageable tasks and gradually increasing the difficulty. In maths, I broke down worked examples into smaller steps and used diagrams and practical examples to explain fractions and equations. In science, I used diagrams and concise notes to make key concepts easier to understand, while for languages I introduced a short daily flashcard routine to help reinforce vocabulary between sessions. When schools closed again during the winter lockdown, I moved the sessions online and used a shared whiteboard to work through problems together. 

Over time, the student's term grade in maths rose from 9/20 to 14/20, their French vocabulary test results improved from sometimes failing to consistently passing.
